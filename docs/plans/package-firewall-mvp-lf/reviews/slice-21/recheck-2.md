## Findings

N1 - Medium - BLOCKING - the "per key" cap is not per tenant: every caller of a deployment shares one key id, so one caller takes all of a key's places
Location: src/http/spool.rs:34-45 (`owner_of(principal.key_id.0, ...)`), src/managed/mod.rs:309 (`key_id: KeyId(0)` for every certificate-admitted principal), src/managed/service_policy.rs:480 (`key_id: snapshot.key_id`, one snapshot per deployment), src/http/registry_read.rs:178-191 (`service_principal` does not validate the credential, it only checks the `Authorization` scheme and forwards it upstream), src/http/limits.rs:162-174 (`spool_seat`).
Failure, service mode: `key` is the same number for every caller, whatever it presents. Four sockets with four junk `Authorization: Basic ...` values and a trickle above the floor (16 KiB/s average, one chunk per 14 s; a declared length of about 7.2 MiB or more earns the whole 15 min deadline) take the key's 4 places. Every later deploy of every legitimate user is 503 for 15 min, then the attacker repeats. The other 4 global places are unreachable by anyone, because there is no second key. The test `junk_credentials_under_one_key_do_not_multiply_its_places` demonstrates exactly this (a fifth, different credential gets 503) and presents it as the intended fairness. The seat count covers the upstream send too, so it holds until the registry answers.
Failure, certificate mode: `key_id` is 0 for all tenants, so one valid tenant running 4 parallel deploys (or 4 slow ones) makes every other tenant's deploy 503, and the whole process admits 4 uploads in total. This is the F2 scenario (a valid key starving other keys) left open in the managed deployment, and the "4 of 8" statement in the evidence and in limits.rs:32 does not hold there.
Correction: count against a real identity: in certificate mode the account or org (`principal.account`, which is distinct per tenant); in service mode the credential only plus a bounded per-client-address count (peer address as `Owner.key`), since the proxy cannot tell valid from junk. Cost: one more field passed to `owner_of` from six call sites (the source-scan test already forces each to call it) and a test with two tenants (certificate principals) where tenant A holds 4 and tenant B still deploys.
Closure check: two certificate principals of different accounts; A holds 4 trickling uploads; B's deploy returns 201, not 503. Service mode: 4 junk credentials from one address cannot make a request from another address 503.
Blocking: yes. The recheck-1 R1 closure test asserts "another key", but no principal in production ever carries a second key in these two modes, so the test cannot show fairness between callers.

N2 - Medium - not blocking - the shared byte budget has no per-owner share: one caller can hold all of it
Location: src/http/spool.rs:~96-106 (`cap = cap.min(spool_budget_total())`), src/http/limits.rs:56 and `Limits::new` (`spool_total = 2 x max_artifact_bytes`, 10 GiB with the shipped 5 GiB).
Failure: a declared or undeclared upload may reserve up to the whole budget (the cap is the budget). Burst the bytes quickly, then trickle: the credit from the burst (bytes / 16 KiB/s seconds, over 15 min from about 14 MiB) lets one chunk every 14 s pass both the floor and the idle limit, so the reservation is held for the whole `BODY_DEADLINE` (900 s). A second upload that finds the budget gone waits `SPOOL_BUDGET_WAIT` 30 s and is 503, for every key and tenant. The attacker needs about 93 Mbit/s for one 10 GiB burst per 15 min (less if the operator lowers max_artifact_bytes: with 200 MiB it needs about 0.5 Mbit/s). Places do not help, the budget is separate.
Why not blocking: it predates this round (the budget and its 15 min deadline were earlier), it needs real bandwidth at the shipped default, and recheck-1 only asked about places. It does defeat the stated residual ("a place is never more than half of all places per key") for bytes.
Correction: reserve at most `budget / 2` (or per-owner share) for one owner, or release bytes of a body whose rate falls to the floor after a burst (cut when the moving rate, not the average since start, is under the floor). Cost: an owner-byte counter next to the owner-seat counter.

N3 - Low - CONFIRMED - the 20 s grace is measured from the start of the body, but the first-chunk wait is 60 s
Location: src/http/spool.rs:84-103 (`FIRST_CHUNK_WAIT` vs `RATE_GRACE`; rate test runs on every chunk including the first).
Scenario: a client that sends headers, then waits 25 s before a small first chunk (Maven wagon after a slow checksum or an `Expect: 100-continue` round trip) gets "the upload is too slow" although the first-chunk wait promised 60 s. The tests use a 10 s grace so they pass. Fix: start the rate clock at the first chunk, or skip the rate test while `length` is that first chunk. Not blocking (a retry succeeds).

N4 - Low - note - a large legitimate slow upload is cut
Location: spool.rs `body_deadline`, `BODY_DEADLINE`. A declared 200 MiB jar at 100 KiB/s needs 2048 s: cut at 900 s ("took too long"). At 228 KiB/s or more it passes; 100 MiB at 100 KiB/s (1024 s) is also cut. The 15 min cap predates this round and is documented; the new floor and the `20 s + 2 x length / 16 KiB/s` formula cut nothing that the cap did not already cut (a 5 MiB body at 100 KiB/s gets 20 + 640 s, finishes in 51 s). Content-Length is checked before seating: maven_routes.rs checks `declared > cap` (413) and `spool_paced` returns `TooLarge` before `spool_seat`, so no seat or byte is taken for an over-cap declaration; an over-declared length only lengthens the deadline up to 15 min (see N1/N2 for what that costs).

N5 - Low - plausible - catalog slots: a hostile tenant needs four registries to hold all four private slots
Location: src/maven/serve.rs:1030-1046. Each credentialed catalog read holds ITS registry's slot (1) and then one of 4 process-wide private slots. One registry therefore holds at most one of the 4; starving all private catalogs needs four slow registries (all configured by the operator or the control plane, not by a caller). A caller can however hold ITS registry's single slot with a junk credential for the fetch time (up to the 120 s document deadline), so other callers of the same registry's catalog get 504 after 30 s: availability of the archetype-catalog document of that registry only (junk credentials are not validated before the fetch). Acceptable; no change needed beyond noting it. Order is slot (own), slot (private), then refresh place: no cycle with `refresh_places` (own refresh slot, then global refresh), and waiting holds nothing global. The semaphore tables are pruned with `strong_count > 1`; `acquire_owned` keeps its Arc, so a held slot is never pruned and the table is bounded by MAX_GATES plus in-flight requests.

## Checks without findings
- Seat leaks (spool.rs, limits.rs): `SpoolSeat` and `SpoolReservation` are values in the future; every `?`/early return (stall, too slow, deadline, TooLarge, storage error, Overloaded), a dropped future (client abort, cancellation), and a panic unwinding drop both; the count map is decremented in `Drop` under a poison-tolerant lock; the seat is taken under the map lock with `try_acquire` (no await while locked, no double count on refusal because the permit is taken before the counters are bumped). `declared == Some(0)` takes no seat; an empty undeclared body takes one and returns it. No leak found.
- (b) row gate (serve.rs:492-504 and call sites 484, 564, 709): held only across store reads and one commit (`record`, `count_project_references_in`, `commit_request_references_in`), never across an upstream fetch, `refresh_places`, `single_flight` or a catalog slot (the gate is taken after `fetch`/`probe_dated`/`module_stays_inside` in `resolve` and `reference_for_file`; in the listing it is taken after the dating `fetched` loop). It nests nothing else except `cell.units` (a std mutex, never held across await), so there is no lock cycle. One project's slow store commit stalls that project only (the wait itself has no timeout, but the hold time is a store operation). A dropped future releases the guard. The table is pruned at 1024 entries with `strong_count > 1` (the clone happens under the table mutex, so a gate in use is never removed); with more than 1024 projects in flight it grows to the in-flight count, then shrinks at the next insert. Atomicity: `resolve`, `reference_for_file` (new row) and the listing recount under the gate, so the bound is exact within one process; across processes it is not (stated, "one firewall owns its store").
- R2 (download.rs:699-741): 403 for an absent `.sha1` is no check; 404/410 unchanged; 401, 5xx, timeouts and 304 fail closed; a present sidecar is compared before pinning. A hostile registry can answer 403 to skip the extra check, which only withdraws a check the registry's own bytes would also fail, same trust as before. Correct.
- R3 (serve.rs:~560-565): an existing row with a stored date at least as late as the unit's is left alone; a later file date than the unit's no longer rewrites on every GET. Correct for the shapes read (the stored date is the max).
- R5: first chunk 60 s, mid-body 15 s (see N3 for the interaction with the rate clock).
- (e) run_tests.py: `default_scratch` resolves `here.parents[2]` to the repository root and creates `<parent>/.probation-scratch/native` on disk, never /tmp; `NATIVE_TEST_SCRATCH` still overrides. It is never deleted (the tests use `TemporaryDirectory(dir=...)`, so only the empty parent remains: negligible).
- Slices 17-20 forwarders (npm registry_action, PyPI, RubyGems, Cargo, NuGet): all six call `spool::owner_of(principal.key_id.0, &headers)`; signatures of `spool`/`spool_with`/`Owner: From<u64>` are unchanged for callers, so no compile or behaviour regression from the code read; N1 and N2 apply to all six equally (shared spool). The recheck-1 evidence reports the five older forwarders' binaries passing; not re-run here.
