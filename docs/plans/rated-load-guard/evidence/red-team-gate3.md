## Answers to the three named questions

**(1) Can the test plan actually fail?** Mostly, but at review time not where it mattered.
`rl18` could fail but not for the case it existed to catch (finding 6); `rl22` could not pass at
all (finding 2); `rl17` failed against a correct implementation (finding 1); `rl11`, two of
`rl7`'s sites and `rl16` could not fail, and `rl16c` went red on correct code (finding 5). All
six are now addressed by C50–C53.

**(2) Is the C46 cancellation fix real?** The diagnosis is verified exactly as written: `send` is
awaited inside the arm *bodies* at `:68` and `:74`, outside the `select!`'s polling, so an
unbounded retry never polls `drain.cancelled()` at `:76`, and `Running::shutdown`
(`src/lib.rs:310-333`) cancels drain then joins with no timeout of its own — it would block
forever. `tokio::time::timeout(DRAIN_DEADLINE, …)` at `:80` genuinely bounds the drain arm,
because `timeout` drops the inner future at an await point. **But** `:80` bounds shutdown only
once the drain arm is *entered*, and entering it is precisely what the hold prevents — so the
claim held under option (a) and was false under option (b). Further: records held at cancellation
are counted only by `Unsent`'s `Drop` at `:209-213`, because `batch.drain(..)` runs before the
first await and the tally at `:95` is blind to the in-flight batch. An implementer who stopped
draining `batch` while leaving `Unsent` armed would **double-count** every held record. C46 now
names `Unsent` as the counting mechanism, mandates option (a), and states that `batch` stays
drained.

**(3) Gate 1 / Gate 2 contradictions.** Three.

- **C49 vs approved Gate 2 `## External`**, which reads in full: "None. No third-party API, no
  webhook, **no new dependency**." C49 adds `proptest` and a committed `proptest-regressions/`,
  argues the point against the criterion precedent, and never mentions the Gate 2 sentence it
  reverses. The `## Files` table's `Cargo.toml` row lists only the `[[bench]]` entry. **Open —
  this is the one finding requiring a user decision, and it is being asked.**
- **`rl18` vs approved Gate 2 `## Fit`** — resolved by C53.
- **C48 vs Gate 2 `## Flow`** ("at least one sink actually configured"): "at most one" is new and
  was unremarked, and the design never said which sink the bench uses — resolved by C54.

C41 and C31 contradict nothing previously settled; C41's defect is under-scoping (finding 4).

## Threat-model gaps the review found

Against `evidence/threat-model-gate3.md`: G3-T3's sole mitigation (`rl22`) was unpassable;
G3-T1's verification carrier (`rl16`) could not discriminate on the target architecture; and
G3-T4/G3-T5's mitigation (C46) was under-specified in a way that reintroduced the threat. C51,
C52 and the C46 restatement close all three.

## Limitations

- `reason`'s construction path was not grounded: a grep hit `max_results_hit: true` and truncated
  before reaching `src/http/`. The reviewer established only that `reason` is a `String` assigned
  unwrapped at `src/http/logging.rs:216`, so finding 2's `reason` limb was "undiscussed premise",
  not "proven unbounded". C51 resolves it on the independent evidence that
  `sf-threat-model` verified every `ApiError` arm supplies a `&'static str`.
- `rl16`'s memory-ordering claim rests on the x86-64 TSO model for this host, not on an executed
  experiment.
- The reviewer did not read `src/config.rs`, `src/http/health.rs`, `src/http/mod.rs` or
  `tests/common/mod.rs`, so "no flaw found" on `rl1`–`rl5`, `rl12`–`rl15` and `rl25` is by
  inspection of the design rather than source-verified.
- Every SMTC leg reported `meta.dirty_build: true`; reads are of current bytes, and no finding
  relied on analysis-derived state.
